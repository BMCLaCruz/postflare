## When to use
- A customer sent a message, review or email describing a concrete result ("I passed my exam", "we stopped losing plants", "I finally finished level 40 with my kid").
- You want to show who the product is for. A good story makes the right reader think "that's me".
- You have a launch or sale coming and want proof in the feed first.

Do not use it without explicit permission, and never paraphrase a quote into something the person did not say.

## Structure
1. **Permission first.** Get a written yes for: their name or handle, the quote, any photo/screenshot, and the platforms it will appear on. Offer anonymity ("a nurse in Leeds").
2. **Line 1: the person and the situation, ≤ 90 characters.** "Mara works 12-hour night shifts and kept forgetting her plants." Specific beats impressive.
3. **The problem in their words.** One or two lines. Include what they tried before; it makes the switch believable.
4. **The turning point.** The one feature or moment that helped. One, not five.
5. **The result.** A concrete outcome with a time frame. If there is a number, use theirs, not an estimate.
6. **The quote.** A short verbatim quote (one to three sentences) in quotation marks, credited as agreed.
7. **Call to action.** One soft, clear next step: "Pocket Plants is free on iOS and Android" plus the link where links work. Put the link in a follow-up where the platform penalises links.

## Platform notes
- **Instagram:** carousel works well: slide 1 the quote on a plain background with their first name, slides 2–4 situation → feature → result, last slide the app. Caption fold at ~125 characters; no clickable links, so "link in bio". Tag the customer only if they agreed. Alt text on every slide.
- **TikTok / Reels / Shorts:** if the customer will film themselves, that beats anything you can make. Otherwise, a screen recording of the feature with their quote as on-screen text. 9:16, first 2 seconds = the situation. Shorts can carry the link in the first comment (follow-up).
- **X:** 280 characters, so story + quote in the main post, link in the first reply (links in the post cut reach and cost more via the API).
- **Bluesky:** 300 graphemes; a link card in a reply or in the post is fine here.
- **Threads:** 500 characters, one topic tag. Links are allowed but a reply keeps the story clean.
- **LinkedIn:** strongest for B2B/SaaS stories. First two lines must work before "…see more" (~210 characters). Link in the first comment. Mention the customer's company only with permission.
- **Facebook:** longer story is fine; link in the first comment.

## Hooks
- **Who + problem:** "{Name} {specific situation} and {specific problem}." — the reader recognises themselves.
- **Quote first:** "'{short striking quote}' — {name}, {role or context}" — a real voice outperforms brand copy.
- **Tried everything:** "{Name} tried {alternative 1} and {alternative 2} before {app}." — makes the switch credible.
- **Result + time:** "{Result} in {time frame}. Here's what {name} changed." — concrete payoff up front.

## Examples
### LinkedIn (Shipnote, a small changelog SaaS)
```
Priya runs a 3-person dev shop and was writing release notes at 11pm on Fridays.

She tried a Notion template, then a Google Doc per client. Both meant copying commit messages by hand into something a client could read.

The thing that changed it: Shipnote drafts the notes from merged pull requests, and she edits instead of writing.

Six weeks in, release notes take her about 15 minutes per client, and two clients now reply to them with feature ideas.

"I stopped dreading Fridays. That's the whole review." — Priya Nair, founder of Lantern Dev

Link to the setup she uses is in the first comment.
```
First comment (follow-up): "Shipnote, free for 2 projects: https://shipnote.example/start"

## Avoid
- Posting without written permission, or tagging/naming beyond what was agreed.
- Edited, merged or invented quotes; fake personas; stock photos posed as the customer.
- Vague praise ("Love this app!!!") as the whole story. If it has no situation and no result, it is not a story.
- Implying the result is typical when it is an outlier.
- Listing every feature; one turning point per story.
- External links in the body on X and LinkedIn; "link in bio" on platforms where links work.

## Checklist
- [ ] Written permission covers name, quote, images and platforms.
- [ ] Line 1 names the person (or anonymised description) and a specific situation.
- [ ] The quote is verbatim and credited as agreed.
- [ ] One feature or turning point, one concrete result with a time frame.
- [ ] No claim of typical results from one case.
- [ ] One clear call to action; link placed per platform (follow-up on X/LinkedIn/Facebook, bio on Instagram).
- [ ] Text fits each platform limit and the fold.
- [ ] Alt text is set on images.
